# Backlink and Authority Strategy

For karkana.com — pyrotechnic atelier, Sivakasi, Tamil Nadu.
Companion to `docs/SECURITY_AUDIT.md` and the SEO work already shipped.

This is a plan, not a claim of results. Nothing here has been executed yet.
Every link tactic below is ranked by effort against the realistic likelihood of
earning the link, and the compliance notes in section 7 are not optional.

---

## 1. What the site currently has to link to

Link building fails when there is nothing worth citing. The site already has
four genuinely linkable assets, and they should be used deliberately:

| Asset | URL | Why it earns links |
| --- | --- | --- |
| The live catalogue | `/module/basic`, `/module/customized`, `/module/personalized` | 138 real SKUs with prices. Aggregators and comparison sites link to structured product data. |
| Machine-readable catalogue | `/llms.txt` | Rare in this vertical. AI-answer engines and tool authors cite sites that publish it. |
| DPDP Act privacy policy | `/privacy-policy` | A correct, current Indian privacy policy is quotable by legal and compliance writers. |
| Sivakasi provenance | homepage, footer | Sivakasi produces the large majority of India's fireworks. Origin stories are locally newsworthy. |

Before chasing links, publish the two things most likely to attract them
naturally, because neither exists yet:

1. **A safety guide.** Firework safety for Indian households and housing
   societies, written for the actual conditions (dense housing, balconies,
   children, pets, Diwali air-quality advisories). This is the single highest
   value page this business can publish. News outlets covering Diwali pollution
   and fire incidents need a citable expert source every October, and there is
   very little credible Indian content on the topic.
2. **A festival buying guide.** What to buy for Diwali by budget, updated
   yearly. Seasonal, searchable, and the natural landing page for the catalogue.

These are not filler. They are the assets that make everything below work.

## 2. Tier 1 — foundational citations (do first, low effort, high certainty)

Consistent Name-Address-Phone across these is worth more than any single
backlink, because it establishes the business as real.

- **Google Business Profile** — Sivakasi address, category "Fireworks
  supplier", opening hours, photos of the workshop. This is the highest value
  listing available and it is free.
- **Justdial, Sulekha, IndiaMART, TradeIndia, ExportersIndia** — the directories
  Indian buyers actually use in this trade.
- **Udyam / MSME registration** — if the business is registered, the Udyam
  listing is authoritative and government-sourced.
- **GST registration details** on the site footer or a `/terms` annexure.
  Business-verification sites scrape this.
- **Local Sivakasi and Virudhunagar district chambers of commerce.**
- **Industry bodies** — The Fireworks Manufacturers Association of India and
  similar trade groups list members.

Expected outcome: 10-20 citations. Mostly `nofollow` or low-authority, but they
are the trust floor. Do not skip them in pursuit of better links.

## 3. Tier 2 — local and regional press (medium effort, high value)

Sivakasi is a genuine news subject every year. Reporters need sources.

**Who to approach:**
- `The Hindu`, `Times of India`, `Deccan Chronicle` — Tamil Nadu editions.
- `Dinamalar`, `Dinakaran`, `Hindu Tamil Thisai` — Tamil dailies with very
  strong Sivakasi coverage and real regional authority.
- `The News Minute`, `India Today`, `Scroll.in` — national digital, all cover
  Diwali manufacturing season.

**What to offer, in order of likely pickup:**
1. **Safety expertise.** A named spokesperson for the annual Diwali safety
   story. Offer the safety guide as the reference document.
2. **Manufacturing process.** Workshop photography and a walkthrough of how a
   cracker is made. Visual stories travel.
3. **Seasonal data.** Anonymous, aggregate order trends — which products sell
   when, how far in advance buyers order. Journalists need numbers and rarely
   have them.
4. **Employment.** Seasonal hiring in Sivakasi is a recurring regional story.

Approach reporters directly by email two to three weeks before Diwali, not
during, when they are already saturated.

## 4. Tier 3 — vertical and adjacent audiences (medium effort)

- **Event and wedding planners.** Personalized and customized fireworks are an
  event product. Planner directories and blogs are a natural fit.
- **Gifting aggregators.** Festival gifting roundups are published every
  October and routinely link to specific products.
- **Housing-society and RWA portals.** Safety guidance for apartment
  communities. The safety guide is the entry point here.
- **Photography communities.** Firework photography guides link to the subject.
- **Sustainable/green crackers.** If any product line is lower-emission,
  environmental publications cover this. Only pursue if the claim is accurate
  and substantiated — see section 7.

---

## 5. Tier 4 — digital PR and data (higher effort, compounding)

Only worth attempting once tiers 1 to 3 are done.

- **Original data.** Publish an annual "State of Sivakasi" report from
  anonymised order data: order volumes by week, average basket, how early
  people order, regional spread. Original data earns links from national press
  in a way that no press release does.
- **Interactive tools.** A budget planner ("what can I buy for ₹2,000") is
  genuinely linkable and trivial to build on the existing catalogue.
- **Supplier and logistics partners.** Anyone the business already works with —
  packaging suppliers, couriers, raw-material vendors — will usually exchange a
  partner-page link.

## 6. What not to do

Recorded explicitly, because these are the tactics that get offered and they
carry real risk:

- **No paid links.** Paid placement without `rel="sponsored"` violates Google's
  spam policies and risks manual action. Given this is a single-domain
  business with no other traffic source, a penalty would be unrecoverable.
- **No private blog networks or link farms.** Detectable, and the sites in this
  vertical are already scrutinised.
- **No article spinning or duplicate syndication.** The catalogue content is
  thin already (see section 8); duplicating it makes that worse.
- **No reciprocal link schemes.** A handful of genuine partnerships is fine; a
  ring is not.
- **No irrelevant directory spam.** Links from unrelated sites signal
  manipulation rather than authority.

## 7. Compliance notes — these constrain the marketing, not just the site

The product category carries legal obligations that directly limit what can be
published and claimed. Treat these as hard constraints on every tactic above:

1. **PESO licensing.** Manufacture and sale of fireworks in India is regulated
   by the Petroleum and Explosives Safety Organisation under the Explosives
   Act 1884 and Explosives Rules 2008. Verify the licence position before any
   PR outreach, because journalists will ask and a licensing gap becoming
   public is a far worse outcome than no coverage.
2. **Supreme Court restrictions.** The court has restricted sale of certain
   categories (notably barium-based "green crackers" claims and several
   high-emission types) and imposed timing restrictions in some jurisdictions.
   Do not market any product whose category is restricted.
3. **Green cracker claims.** "Green" and "low-emission" are regulated claims
   tied to CSIR-NEERI formulations and specific logos. Use them only if the
   product genuinely carries the certification.
4. **Advertising restrictions.** ASCI code and the Drugs and Magic Remedies Act
   aside, several states restrict firework advertising outright. Check
   state-specific rules before running any paid campaign.
5. **DPDP Act 2023.** Any lead-capture or outreach list built from this
   activity falls under the same consent obligations already documented in
   `/privacy-policy`.
6. **Age and safety claims.** The site already states 18+ and adult
   supervision. Marketing must not contradict that.

## 8. On-page work that must land first

Backlinks amplify whatever they point at. Two catalogue gaps should be closed
before outreach, because a journalist or buyer landing on an empty product page
does not convert into a link:

- **All 138 products have an empty `description` field.** Product pages
  currently fall back to a generated sentence. Thin pages earn few links and
  rank poorly. Writing real descriptions is the highest-leverage content task
  in the repository.
- **The `/module/personalized` page has zero products.** The module is linked
  from the footer and homepage but has nothing in it. Either populate it or
  remove it from navigation until it is populated; an empty category linked
  from the homepage is a poor landing page for any inbound link.
- **Categories are inconsistent** (`Other` 12 items vs `Others` 20). Worth
  normalising before any structured-data or comparison-site submission.
- **No product photography for many items.** Image-led links and social shares
  need images.

## 10. Measuring it

Track monthly, and do not chase raw link counts:

- **Referring domains** (not total backlinks) in Google Search Console and one
  third-party index. Referring domains is the metric that moves rankings.
- **Non-branded organic impressions and clicks** for the guide pages.
- **Ranking position** for a fixed set of intent queries (for example "buy
  crackers online Sivakasi", "diwali crackers delivery", "personalized
  fireworks").
- **Direct traffic and phone calls**, which are the actual business outcome and
  often move before rankings do.

Search Console verification requires DNS or file verification once the site is
deployed; `robots.txt` and `sitemap.xml` are already published and correct, so
no further site changes are needed to begin tracking.
